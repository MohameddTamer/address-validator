# Design — Address Validator certificate

## Concept

Validation is an act of judgment, so the report is a certificate, not a
dashboard. One address per sheet, given room to be inspected the way an
inspector would hold it up to the light: mostly silence, a short list of
findings set like lines of a letter, and the verdict delivered once, at the
end, with a stamp. Japanese *ma* — negative space as an active material — is
the operating principle. What is not on the page is doing as much work as
what is.

## Palette

- Bone paper: `#F4F1EA`
- Sumi ink (all text that matters): `#1A1A18`
- Stone (labels, metadata, the annex): `#8A8578`
- Hairline: `#D8D2C4`
- Vermillion `#C73E1D`: the stamp, the seal mark, and a small tick beside a
  failed finding. A handful of appearances per page, never a theme colour.
  If vermillion shows up twice on one sheet, something is wrong with the
  sheet, not with the palette.

## Type

**Newsreader** light (300) for everything read slowly — the certificate
title, the inspected address itself, the findings, the verdict sentence in
italic. **IBM Plex Mono** at 10.5–12.5px, letterspaced and uppercased, for
labels, order numbers and the annex — the paperwork voice, kept quiet.
Tabular numerals throughout. The pairing is the whole design: a serif that
sounds like a person judging, and a mono that sounds like a form being filed.

## Signature move

The verdict stamp. Each sheet ends above a single hairline with the sentence
("This address may ship." / "Held. A human should look before anything is
dispatched.") and the vermillion seal settling in beside it — one 600ms
fade-and-scale, the only motion on the page, which is exactly why it means
something. A stamp is a decision a person stands behind; the page borrows
that weight and spends it nowhere else.

Below the sheets, rows that never reached inspection (malformed, duplicated)
are set aside in a small mono annex. Even the rejects are filed calmly.

## Deliberate avoidances

- No charts. The batch findings are a list of quiet lines with counts;
  a bar chart would shout over a document whose value is judgment.
- No boxes, no cards, no rules except the one hairline above each verdict
  and the one above the annex.
- No centred hero symmetry; the composition is off-centre and margin-led,
  and the margins follow the same spacing rhythm on every sheet so the
  emptiness reads as composed, not unfinished.
- No traffic-light status colours. A failed check is ink with a vermillion
  tick; the stamp carries the verdict alone.
- No motion besides the stamp. A 600ms fade is the loudest event allowed.
